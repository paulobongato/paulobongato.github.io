Measuring across instead of around. The chart measures a shirt lying flat, so the body input does the same — armpit to armpit in a straight line. A torso is an oval, not a folded sheet, so that straight-across reading is roughly a third of the way round, not half: about 12.6″ across for a 39″ chest. The sheet converts before comparing, and shows both numbers in the panel header. Worth knowing: because the conversion multiplies by about three, every 1⁄4″ you are off measuring becomes 3⁄4″ of chest, so keep the tape genuinely straight and level.

How width becomes a size. The chart’s width is the flat pit‑to‑pit, so doubling it gives the garment’s chest circumference — adult M at 20″ is a 40″ garment. Against a body chest that gives ease, and ease is the whole recommendation, and this sheet uses one regular fit: 1.5–4.7″. The chart’s own instruction — size up when you fall between two — is applied on close calls.

Shoulder and sleeve are derived, not charted. Both rows are marked EST, from standard tee drafting ratios against the charted width (shoulder ≈ 0.88 of width for adults, sleeve ≈ 0.42). That is how a grader would infer them, but it is inference, and production samples will differ.

The kids lengths run short. The adult rows land within half an inch of where a hem should sit — that grade is sound. The kids rows sit 2–3″ above the hip across most of the range, so those tees read cropped on a child tall enough to need that width. Reported rather than corrected: length is weighted lightly and chest ease drives the pick.
